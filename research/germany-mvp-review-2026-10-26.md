## Findings that can support the MVP

### Master's admission and student residence

- A student must be admitted to a state-recognised higher-education institution; sufficient living support and the programme's language evidence also matter. Language level is programme-specific (the federal portal says B2 is usual for some programmes), not a universal German-language prerequisite. The portal's checklist is a summary, so exact admission eligibility, language tests, enrollment evidence, and consular documents need to be checked for the chosen programme and German mission. [Federal study visa page](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-recognition/visa-types/studying) and [study requirements overview](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/study-vocational-training/studying-in-germany)
- The portal says a study residence permit is usually first issued for two years; extension can be requested if the degree is not yet completed. Student work is capped at 140 full days or 280 half days a year (or 20 hours per week in the visa page's summary); student auxiliary jobs are treated separately. Do not model student work permission as post-study work permission. [Federal study visa page](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-recognition/visa-types/studying)
- For the study title, the federal 2026 financial proof benchmark is €992 net monthly / €11,904 annually. A blocked account is one route; a scholarship or declaration of commitment may also establish funds. Blocked-account deposits are released in monthly amounts. Treat €11,904 as a means-of-support proof balance, not tuition, a fee, or an additive cost on top of the student's living expenses. The amount is updated by year and the portal says individual assessment still applies. [2026 federal financial-security table](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/pdf-financial-security-living-expenses) and [federal study visa page](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-recognition/visa-types/studying)

### Graduation and job search

- After successful completion of qualifying study in Germany under §16b/§16c, the applicant may apply for a separate §20 job-search residence permit. It is issued for up to 18 months, requires secured livelihood, and cannot be extended beyond the statutory maximum. The federal summary says any occupation is allowed while looking for skilled work. This is an application and status change, not automatic conversion on graduation. [Residence Act §20](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__20.html), [federal study visa page](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-recognition/visa-types/studying), and [federal post-graduation guidance](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/study-vocational-training/studies-in-germany/prospects-after)
- **No source-backed euro proof amount for the §20 livelihood condition was identified in the material checked.** Do not reuse the €11,904 study balance or the opportunity-card/jobseeker €1,091 monthly figure. Flag this amount and acceptable evidence for the graduate stage as unresolved; obtain the current local authority checklist for a real case.

### Skilled employment / EU Blue Card

- A German degree may support a skilled-worker permit under §18b for suitable qualified employment, but an offer alone does not establish status. Job fit, qualification, recognition/licensing where relevant, and the competent authority's decision must be assessed. Keep this as a separate permit application after the job-search stage. [Residence Act §18b](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__18b.html) and [federal skilled-worker / Blue Card overview](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/working-in-germany)
- The federal qualified-professional guidance also lists an age-over-45 condition for a first work permit: in 2026, gross annual salary of at least €55,770 or evidence of adequate pension provision. The new work checklist does not assess this condition; age alone is insufficient to determine applicability, so retain a case-specific authority check. [Federal work visa for qualified professionals](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/work-qualified-professionals)
- The 2026 Blue Card path requires a comparable qualification and specific job offer of at least six months, matching the qualification; regulated work may need a licence. Thresholds are **€50,700 gross/year standard**, **€45,934.20** for listed shortage occupations with Federal Employment Agency approval, or **€45,934.20** for a new entrant whose last degree/equivalent is less than three years old (all occupations; BA approval). These are alternative employment-permit salary criteria, not study costs or living-funds proof. Refresh thresholds annually. [Federal EU Blue Card requirements](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card)
- The general §18b skilled-worker route and the Blue Card have different conditions; do not force every qualifying job through Blue Card thresholds. [§18b Residence Act](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__18b.html), [Blue Card source](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card)

### Settlement permit

- The German-graduate settlement route under §18c(1) is a distinct facilitated track: after at least two years holding an eligible skilled-worker title (§§18a, 18b, 18d or 18g), the graduate must also have a permitted job, 24 months of statutory pension contributions, B1 German, basic legal/social knowledge, and sufficient living space. The public-facing guidance also lists sufficient livelihood. Thus “two years after graduation” is an incomplete shortcut: the relevant two years are in qualifying skilled-worker status, alongside the other requirements. [Residence Act §18c](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__18c.html) and [federal settlement guidance](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/living-permanently/settlement-permit)
- Blue Card holders instead have the §18c(2) settlement track: qualified employment and pension contributions for 27 months with A1 German, or 21 months with B1, plus the other stated requirements. Keep this alternative separate from the German-graduate §18c(1) two-year/24-month contribution path. It is not a guaranteed grant at a timer boundary. [§18c Residence Act](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__18c.html), [federal settlement guidance](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/living-permanently/settlement-permit)
- The regular settlement track differs (the federal summary describes three years on specified titles and 36 pension-contribution months for skilled workers). A separate §9 rule credits study time only partially for §9 residence-period calculations; do not apply that rule to erase or shorten the separate §18c employment-title / contribution requirements. See consolidated [Residence Act](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/BJNR195010004.html), §§9(4), 18c.
- Naturalisation/citizenship eligibility and processing are outside this review. Do not project a passport date from the settlement stages.

A first-year cash-flow view needs the exact school/city, billing dates, insurance route, and one-time local charges. No dependants' budget is sourced.

## Actionable product gaps

Primary integration review independently retrieved the federal post-graduation, Blue Card, qualified-professionals and settlement pages on 26 October 2026. These confirm the distinctions used in the new partial checklist. The primary retry of the §18c statutory endpoint timed out; the statutory retrieval above is the researcher's evidence, not a successful primary re-fetch. No applied catalog batch or whole-route verification status was changed.

1. Require named university/programme, cohort/start date, admission, tuition schedule, language requirements, location, and provider-specific semester fee before reporting a study cost estimate.
2. Track funds-proof balance independently from expenses and require an explicit 12-month study-support evidence path; preserve alternate scholarship/declaration options.
3. Add dated insurance-context inputs (insurer, age, enrolment/status, exemption/family coverage, children, validity dates). Surface the TK rows only as a conditional tariff lookup; do not auto-add to a total.
4. Keep completion evidence and job-search application as a conditional transition; flag the §20 funds amount and local filing evidence as unknown until separately verified.
5. For employment, branch §18b and Blue Card. Capture contract start/length, qualification-job fit, regulated-profession licence, salary period/currency, shortage/young-professional category, and BA approval as applicable.
6. For settlement, preserve legal status and exact date intervals, pension contribution months, job, language, livelihood, housing and civic-knowledge evidence; do not use study duration as the §18c skilled-worker period or blend two settlement tracks.
7. Refresh annual euro thresholds and confirm appointment/processing and fees with the relevant mission/foreigners authority. Keep naturalisation outside this route until reviewed independently.
